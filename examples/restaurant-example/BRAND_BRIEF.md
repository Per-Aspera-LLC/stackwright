# Brand Brief — Ember Kitchen

## Core Identity
- **Company**: Ember Kitchen
- **What We Do**: Neighborhood wood-fire restaurant serving seasonal, farm-to-table cuisine for dinner nightly and weekend brunch
- **Audience**: Portland locals seeking a special-but-not-stuffy dining experience. Date nights, celebrations, family dinners.
- **Value Proposition**: Honest food cooked over fire — seasonal ingredients, no shortcuts, always worth it
- **Tagline**: Good fire. Good food. Good people.

## Brand Personality
- **Adjectives**: Warm, Honest, Inviting, Craft-driven, Unpretentious
- **Tone**: Conversational and genuine — like a host who genuinely loves feeding people
- **Emotional Target**: Anticipation and comfort — "I can already smell it" and "I feel at home here"
- **Voice**: Evocative but grounded. Sensory details. No food-blog clichés. "Pork shoulder slow-smoked for 12 hours" not "artisanal hand-crafted heritage pork"

## Visual Direction
- **Color Mood**: Warm terracotta and cream — like the inside of a well-loved kitchen. Earthy, inviting, not trendy.
- **Preferred Colors**: Terracotta (#C1440E), warm cream (#FBF5E6), deep charcoal text (#2C2824)
- **Typography Feel**: A warm serif for headings (human, crafted), clean sans for readability
- **Layout Density**: Airy and welcoming — let the food breathe. Big sections, generous whitespace.
- **Imagery Style**: Would normally use food photography; we use descriptive copy and icons for this reference example

## Content Strategy
- **Pages Needed**: Home, Menu, Reservations
- **Primary CTA**: Make a Reservation
- **Hero Message**: Good fire. Good food. Good people.

## Differentiation
- **Competitors**: Upscale downtown restaurants (stuffy), fast-casual (too casual), other farm-to-table spots (trendy/precious)
- **How We're Different**: The fire is real, the sourcing is honest, and nobody will make you feel underdressed

## Brand Guidelines
- Never use "artisan", "curated", or "elevated" — describe the thing, not the marketing
- Mention farmers and producers by name when possible
- Reservations are preferred but walk-ins welcome at the bar
- Weekend brunch is a big deal — families, celebrations, a longer pace
